**Key Principles (GIRFT)**

The GIRFT guide provides high-level follow-up protocol considerations for standardising outpatient pathways. It is intended to support local teams in initiating review and redesign of follow-up pathways.

CRITICAL GIRFT PRINCIPLE — Discharge first, PIFU second:

  - The initial clinic appointment in secondary care should be arranged with enough time and patient information given with the AIM OF AVOIDING the need for further face-to-face follow-up appointments.

  - PIFU should ideally be LIMITED TO 6-12 MONTHS where used.

  - Where PIFU is requested, clinical triage is advisable to ensure the most effective review is undertaken.

  - Specialist nurse services can be used to optimise outpatient pathway flow.

**Cardiology – Atrial Fibrillation (AF)**

**Pre-Attendance (Primary Care)**

Patient presents in primary care with symptoms consistent with AF or incidental finding of AF. Initial assessment, history and examination. Check pulse, BP, ECG, FBC, U&E. Review and address alcohol intake, BMI if relevant. Refer to GIRFT Advice and Guidance.

**First Presentation (New Patient Appointment)**

Could be with a specialist nurse or consultant. As per atrial fibrillation pathway. Aim for day case unless clinically indicated.

**PIFU Guidance for AF**

  - PIFU should be used where appropriate, particularly where symptoms require reassessment — this can be delivered via (nurse-led) PIFU/SPoA.

  - PIFU should ideally be limited to 6-12 months.

  - Post successful ablation: while ECGs/ECG monitoring and reassessment of symptoms are required, ROUTINE FOLLOW-UP IN A DOCTOR CLINIC SHOULD NOT BE. PIFU is often adequate.

  - Post successful cardioversion of AF: while ECGs and reassessment of symptoms are required, routine follow-up in a doctor clinic should not be.

**Notes on AF Pathway**

  - The initial clinic appointment in secondary care should be arranged with enough time and patient information given with the aim of AVOIDING the need for further face-to-face follow-up appointments.

  - Specialist nurse services can be used to optimise pathway flow: history taking, supporting education and medication optimisation, education around lifestyle factors, answering ad hoc queries outside of appointments.

  - AF management: See GIRFT pathway.

  - Palpitations management: See GIRFT pathway.

**Cardiology – Aortic Stenosis**

**Pre-Attendance (Primary Care)**

Patient presents with symptoms consistent with aortic stenosis. Initial assessment, history and examination including auscultation. Check pulse, BP, ECG, Pro BNP, FBC, U&E. Review and address alcohol intake, BMI if relevant. Echo. Symptoms may include: chest pain or tightness (especially with exertion), shortness of breath, fatigue, dizziness (on exertion) or fainting (syncope). Refer to GIRFT Advice and Guidance.

**First Presentation (New Patient Appointment)**

Clinical review and review of images patient within Valve Clinic. Confirmatory tests as required (e.g. CT, echo modalities). If need for intervention suspected, refer to MDT. If no current requirement for intervention, patient discharged or valve surveillance.

**MDT Discussion**

MDT meeting to discuss whether intervention is required and type: Surgical, TAVI, Medical management, or Palliative.

**Follow-Up**

  - Valvular heart disease follow-up should be PHYSIOLOGIST-LED within a focused echo surveillance clinic according to agreed local protocols.

  - Assessing post-TAVI outcomes: assesses symptoms, can be done virtually with additional data from physiology-led echo valve surveillance at appropriate intervals.

  - Wound review may rarely be needed and can be done virtually (patients sending photographs) in the majority of cases.

  - Post surgical follow-up.

**PIFU Guidance for Aortic Stenosis**

  - If symptoms require reassessment, this can be delivered via nurse-led PIFU/SPoA.

  - PIFU should ideally be limited to 6-12 months.

  - Reviewing medication should NOT require face-to-face discussion.

  - Patients should be provided pros/cons regarding their options, but this should not normally require face-to-face discussion.

**Cardiology – Chest Pain**

**Pre-Attendance (Primary Care)**

Patient presents with symptoms consistent with chest pain with suspected cardiac origin. Initial assessment, history and examination. Check pulse, BP, ECG, Troponin, FBC, U&E. Review and address alcohol intake, BMI if relevant. Echocardiogram. Address lifestyle factors. Refer to ACP-led RACPC if symptoms and risk factors suggest high risk but further non-invasive assessment needed. If appropriate, commence anti-anginal medication and consider secondary prevention.

**First Presentation (New Patient Appointment)**

Clinical assessment: ECG, CTCA or functional imaging. If appropriate, commence anti-anginal medication and consider secondary prevention. If high-risk features: consider coronary angiogram +/- invasive imaging/invasive physiological assessment and stent. MDT discussion as appropriate.

**Follow-Up**

Assessing post-PCI outcome: assesses symptoms and can be done virtually.

**PIFU Guidance for Chest Pain**

  - If symptoms require reassessment, this can be delivered via nurse-led PIFU/SPoA.

  - PIFU should ideally be limited to 6-12 months.

  - Specialist nurse services can support pathway flow: history taking, education and medication optimisation, lifestyle factors, answering ad hoc queries.

**Cardiology – Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB)**

**Pre-Attendance**

Patient presents with an incidental or previously unknown finding of LBBB as an isolated abnormality on the ECG. This includes incidental findings from primary care, surgical pre-admission or specialties other than cardiology.

Initial assessment, history and examination. Check pulse, BP, ECG, FBC, U&E, NT-pro BNP. Request echocardiogram unless a recent echo is available. If acute coronary syndrome (ACS) is suspected, follow GIRFT chest pain pathway. Review and address BMI, alcohol intake and hypertension if relevant.

**Triage / Advice and Guidance**

  - Follow the GIRFT Cardiology Advice and Guidance template for LBBB.

  - If ACS is suspected, follow GIRFT chest pain pathway.

  - LBBB WITHOUT concerning history and investigation, including echo, does NOT require referral to cardiology.

  - Where referral is required, treatment should be initiated and referral made to the most appropriate clinic (e.g. RACPC/specialist valve/heart failure).

**Follow-Up**

  - Surveillance may be required if the patient has valve disease or heart failure.

  - For other conditions, PIFU should be used where appropriate.

  - PIFU should ideally be limited to 6-12 months.

  - Surveillance by specialised cardiac physiology team may be appropriate.

**Cardiology – Heart Failure**

**Pre-Attendance (Primary or Secondary Care)**

Follow: Chronic heart failure in adults: diagnosis and management (NICE NG106). Initial assessment, history and examination. Complete a frailty assessment to see whether cardiology input is appropriate. Initial investigations: observations, 12 lead ECG and heart failure blood tests (FBC, U&E, LFT, TSH, iron studies and NT-pro BNP). The NT-pro BNP level should be considered in context of comorbidities (e.g. age, renal dysfunction, presence of AF). Review and address BMI, alcohol intake and hypertension. Initiate medication (e.g. diuretic) if clearly indicated.

**Triage / Advice and Guidance**

  - Request echocardiogram if signs and symptoms of heart failure in addition to elevated NT-pro BNP.

  - Follow the GIRFT Cardiology Advice and Guidance template for heart failure with preserved ejection fraction.

  - A hospital clinic appointment will RARELY be needed for HFpEF.

**Referral to Cardiology**

Patients with NT-pro BNP greater than 2000 should be referred and seen within TWO WEEKS with results of all tests (including echo) available at the clinic appointment.

**Follow-Up**

  - Optimisation of medication should be performed by community heart failure team.

  - Community and hospital heart failure teams should have agreed Service Level Agreements (SLAs).

  - Once patient fully investigated and on optimal treatment, PIFU should be used where appropriate.

  - Where PIFU is requested, clinical triage is advisable to ensure the most effective review is undertaken.

**General GIRFT Outpatient Principles**

**Discharge First**

The GIRFT guide strongly emphasises that reducing unnecessary follow-up appointments is the primary goal:

  - Identify patients who can obviously be discharged and those clearly who cannot.

  - Identifying patients for DISCHARGE (i.e. those that do not need follow-up at all) is MORE IMPORTANT than identifying patients for PIFU.

  - The whole idea is that you reduce follow-ups in clinic so that there is more space for seeing new patients — therefore reducing waiting lists.

**Role of Specialist Nurses**

Specialist nurse services can be used to optimise the outpatient pathway flow across all cardiology conditions:

  - History taking as part of initial appointment.

  - Supporting education and medication optimisation.

  - Education around lifestyle factors.

  - Answering ad hoc queries outside of appointments.

**Virtual Consultations**

Reviewing medication should not require face-to-face discussion. Post-procedure outcomes (post-PCI, post-TAVI) can typically be assessed virtually. Wound review can usually be done virtually (patients sending photographs).

**Why Use PIFU in Heart Failure?**

There are an estimated 920,000 people living with heart failure in the UK and around 200,000 new diagnoses every year. Heart failure is a common cause of unplanned hospital admission with more than 100,000 admissions each year.

As heart failure is a long-term condition, patients should be placed on a CONTINUOUS PIFU pathway so they can access the heart failure MDT for ongoing help and support. This avoids the need to go back to their GP for another referral, which may unnecessarily delay care. PIFU enables the patient to connect back with the heart failure MDT when they need help because their condition has an uncertain disease trajectory.

PIFU is NOT an alternative to discharge — suitable patients should be discharged using routine trust guidelines.

**Patient Selection**

**Heart Failure Patients Suitable for PIFU**

Heart failure type:

  - Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).

  - Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).

  - Heart failure with mid-range reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF).

Regardless of type, all patients should be STABLE and have MEDICINES OPTIMISED before transfer to PIFU.

Patient details:

  - Aged 18 and over.

  - Patient or carer understands and accepts the meaning and principles of a continuous PIFU pathway.

  - Patient or carer understands and feels confident using the heart failure traffic light system for managing symptoms and knows how and when to call for help.

**Patients NOT Suitable for PIFU**

Heart failure criteria:

  - Patients with outstanding investigations or decisions to be made regarding further options for care.

  - Patients who are potential candidates for device therapy (CRT and/or ICD) according to NICE/ESC guidelines but are still awaiting further diagnostic investigations and/or therapeutic interventions.

  - Heart failure patients with UNCONTROLLED SYMPTOMS.

  - Heart failure patients being managed on an END-OF-LIFE care pathway.

Patient details:

  - Patients unable to contact the service in a timely way (e.g. lack of access to telephone/internet).

  - Patients with low levels of knowledge, skills and confidence to manage their follow-up care and/or no carer support.

**Traffic Light System for Symptom Management**

Heart failure symptoms on a continuous PIFU pathway are managed using a traffic light system:

| Level | Symptoms | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RED — Take Action (NOT suitable for PIFU service)** | Severe breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations and syncope, symptoms of infection, other medical conditions worsening, transient loss of consciousness, feeling confused about medication, diarrhoea or vomiting for more than 24 hours. | Urgent advice from GP or dial 999 or NHS 111 out of hours. |
| **AMBER — Stay Alert (Suitable for PIFU)** | Weight increase by 4 pounds/2 kg over 3 days AND feeling more breathless than usual, leg swelling, breathless at night, needing more pillows to sleep, less active than usual, feeling muddled, other conditions worsening, carer unwell and unable to look after the patient. | Contact the PIFU service for advice and consider a review with the HF MDT. |
| **GREEN — Keep Watch (Suitable for PIFU)** | Weight has not increased by 4 pounds/2 kg over 3 days AND no more breathless than usual, ankles no more swollen than normal, all medical conditions are stable, active and mobile as normal, carer well and able to look after patient. | Contact the PIFU service for advice if necessary. |

**Target Response Times**

Target response time: 2 WORKING DAYS from patient calling the PIFU helpline to speaking to a member of the heart failure MDT.

The responding clinician decides if the patient requires any tests and/or a consultation. Response time delays or lack of clinic capacity should be raised urgently with the heart failure clinical lead and outpatient performance manager.

Patients experiencing RED flag symptoms should seek urgent advice from GP or dial 999 or NHS 111 out of hours — NOT contact the PIFU service.

**Designing a Continuous PIFU Model for Heart Failure**

**Key Considerations**

  - The decision to put a patient on a continuous PIFU pathway lies with the heart failure clinician in charge, in consultation with the patient.

  - All patients on a PIFU pathway must be placed on a REGISTER so they can be tracked.

  - If a patient needs to be removed from PIFU, this should involve the dementia team, care home, social prescribers, and pharmacists as well as the HF MDT — feeding back to the clinical records and tracking systems.

  - Carers should be able to contact the heart failure PIFU team on the patient's behalf.

  - Ambulance teams should be aware of the PIFU service and how it impacts on patient pathways.

  - If a patient is placed on a ReSPECT or end-of-life pathway, ALL teams involved in care should be informed, and the patient should be removed from the PIFU pathway.

**Technology**

  - Consider use of Telehealth to identify early signs of decompensation and aid medication optimisation.

  - Consider use of mobile apps for HF to reinforce criteria for PIFU with clear points of contact, Red/Amber/Green signs to prompt self-referral, mood monitoring, medication prompts.

  - Remote monitoring through devices which detect fluid overload and detect and treat arrhythmias.

  - Around 25% of HF patients are followed up regularly in pacing clinics (remotely/face-to-face) enabling rapid detection of new AF, decreased biventricular pacing, change in symptoms.

**Shared Decision-Making Must Cover**

  - Why the patient is suitable for PIFU.

  - Comprehensive information and education of the traffic light system for managing symptoms.

  - How to contact the PIFU helpline with appropriate leaflets, websites and phone numbers.

  - Expected timescales for a response from the heart failure MDT if the patient calls for help.

  - How a continuous PIFU pathway works between primary and secondary care.

  - How PIFU links with community-based heart failure services such as cardiac rehabilitation.

  - How PIFU links with remote monitoring devices or additional timed follow-up appointments.

  - How PIFU works with the heart failure care plan.

  - Clarification that the patient can change their mind about PIFU and revert to traditional follow-up.

Shared decision-making conversations, copies of care plans and remote monitoring services should be recorded in the medical records and a copy sent to the GP.

**Health Inequalities**

About 10% of people with heart failure have significant cognitive impairment that can prevent engagement with the PIFU process. Where appropriate, involve carers to support the patient.

Trusts have a legal responsibility to complete their own Equality and Health Inequalities Impact Assessment (EHIA) for PIFU services.

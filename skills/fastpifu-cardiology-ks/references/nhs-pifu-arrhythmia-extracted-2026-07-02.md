**NHS England – Setting Up PIFU Services for People with Arrhythmia**

**General Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria**

PIFU should never be used as an alternative to clinical discharge. Suitable patients should be discharged using routine service guidelines.

**Inclusion Criteria**

  - Aged 18 and over.

  - Patient or carer understands the principles of a PIFU pathway and feels confident using it.

  - Regardless of the type of arrhythmia, patient is stable, and their medicines have been optimised.

**Exclusion Criteria**

  - Arrhythmia criteria: Patients with outstanding investigations or decisions still to be made regarding further options for care.

  - Cannot easily contact the service (e.g. lack of access to telephone/internet).

  - Low level of knowledge, skills and confidence to manage their follow-up care and/or no carer support.

**Condition-Specific PIFU Decisions**

**Atrial Fibrillation (AF)**

**Persistent AF — Rate Control**

Persistent AF managed with rate control and good left ventricular (LV) function: PIFU is NOT appropriate — these patients can be discharged post rate control.

**Persistent AF — Rhythm Control (Medical)**

Persistent AF managed medically with rhythm control: PIFU is NOT suitable — these patients should be offered long-term follow-up or shared care with their GP.

**Persistent AF — Post Successful Ablation (Good LV Function)**

Symptomatic with good LV function, managed successfully with ablation: patients can be offered ONE follow-up appointment and TIME-LIMITED PIFU for 2 years.

**Persistent AF — Poor LV Function Post Ablation**

Associated with poor LV function, managed successfully with ablation: PIFU is NOT suitable. Recurrence of AF and LV impairment often occurs well before symptoms. Offer long-term follow-up with electrophysiology or heart failure service.

**Paroxysmal AF (PAF)**

**Asymptomatic with Good LV Function and Good Rate Control**

PIFU is NOT appropriate — these patients can be discharged.

**Symptomatic, Managed with Class 1 or Class 3 Anti-Arrhythmic Drug**

PIFU is NOT suitable — offer long-term follow-up or shared care with GP. Patients managed with class 2 or 4 anti-arrhythmic drug may be suitable for continuous PIFU or discharged.

**Symptomatic, Managed Successfully with Ablation**

Offer a 3-6 month review and then TIME-LIMITED PIFU for 2 years.

**PAF/Persistent AF treated with AV Node Ablation + Pacemaker**

Echocardiogram at 3-6 months, then discharged to follow-up at pacing clinic and, if appropriate, heart failure clinic.

**Typical Atrial Flutter**

  - Symptom free and in sinus rhythm or stable well-controlled flutter with stable LV function: DISCHARGE at 3-6 months.

  - Persistent significant LV dysfunction: may need ongoing care under a heart failure clinic.

  - Co-existing AF: managed under the AF pathway.

**Atypical Atrial Flutter / Atrial Tachycardia**

  - Successfully treated: DISCHARGE or refer back to the referring team after 6 months.

  - With recurrent atrial arrhythmias: consider suitability for a LONG-TERM PIFU pathway.

**Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)**

  - Managed successfully with ablation: seen once post ablation + ECG, then DISCHARGE or timed PIFU at clinician's discretion.

  - Managed successfully with medication: consider LONG-TERM PIFU.

**Pre-Excitation (managed without electrophysiological study)**

Consider LONG-TERM PIFU with the option of periodic outpatient review.

**Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)**

**Structurally Normal Heart**

  - Medically managed: consider TIMED PIFU for 3 years at physician's discretion.

  - Managed successfully with ablation: see once post ablation + ECG, then DISCHARGE or TIME-LIMITED PIFU for 2 years.

**Ischaemic Cardiomyopathy (regardless of device status)**

Once optimised (medically or with ablation): NOT suitable for PIFU. Move to shared care with heart failure team and pacing/device team and GP.

**Non-Ischaemic Cardiomyopathy**

Long-term follow-up or shared care with inherited cardiomyopathy team.

**Inherited Channelopathy**

Long-term follow-up from the inherited channelopathy clinic.

**Ventricular Ectopy**

  - Asymptomatic high burden with normal ventricular function: offer repeat echocardiogram at 1 year, then DISCHARGE if no clinical concerns.

  - Asymptomatic/symptomatic causing impaired LV function, successfully treated with ablation: offer 2-year follow-up, then DISCHARGE if LV function fully recovered.

  - Symptomatic treated with ablation, beta blocker or calcium channel blocker: consider TIME-LIMITED PIFU at physician's discretion.

**Cardiac Rhythm Management (CRM) Devices In Situ**

  - Not under arrhythmia service: not on PIFU; refer using normal referral pathways if symptoms develop.

  - On PIFU (already under arrhythmia service): remote monitoring can be used in PIFU review to identify cause of symptoms.

**Other Arrhythmias (e.g. Inappropriate Sinus Tachycardia, Low Burden SVE)**

DISCHARGE where appropriate but may be suitable for PIFU at clinician's discretion.

**PIFU Pathway Types**

**Timed PIFU Pathways**

Patient is offered PIFU for a predetermined time (e.g. 2 years) before being discharged back to their GP.

**Continuous PIFU Pathways**

Patient with a long-term condition requires ongoing access to a specialist team to manage their condition indefinitely.

**Target Response Times**

A patient contacting the PIFU helpline for the arrhythmia service should expect a response within 2 WORKING DAYS. The arrhythmia team will decide if and how soon the patient requires any tests and/or a consultation.

Response time delays or lack of clinic capacity should be raised urgently with the service lead. Any delays should be communicated to the patient/carer. If there are safety concerns, an alternative plan should be created.

**Technology**

Where appropriate, technological devices should be used, for example:

  - Remote monitoring for patients with implantable cardiac rhythm devices.

  - Phone apps or supplementary tools (e.g. Kardia) that the patient can use to record ECGs and send these direct to the clinical team.

**Shared Decision-Making Discussions Must Cover**

  - What PIFU is and why the patient is suitable for it.

  - How and when to contact the arrhythmia service, with phone numbers and leaflets.

  - The time within which a patient/carer calling for help can expect a response.

  - How a PIFU pathway works between primary and secondary care.

  - How PIFU connects with any remote monitoring or additional timed follow-up appointments.

  - Clarification that the patient can change their mind about PIFU.

  - Clarification that if the patient is unwell and cannot immediately contact their local PIFU team, they should still access primary care and/or emergency services.

**NHS England – PIFU and Surveillance Imaging for Mild to Moderate Heart Valve Disease**

Data extracted: 2 July 2026

Published: 1 August 2023, Updated: 15 December 2023

**Introduction**

This guide outlines a PIFU model of care for patients with mild to moderate heart valve disease (HVD) or following valve intervention who require ongoing surveillance imaging but not necessarily frequent clinician review.

Patients with HVD are commonly asymptomatic and often require surveillance imaging (predominantly echocardiography) as part of their routine management. A heart valve PIFU pathway works effectively alongside a surveillance imaging pathway, enabling the patient to self-manage but also have the safety net of being able to contact the heart valve team if they develop concerning symptoms.

IMPORTANT: These pathways are NOT appropriate for patients with severe valve disease or symptomatic disease.

PIFU should NEVER be used as an alternative to clinical discharge. Suitable patients should be discharged following routine trust guidelines.

**Patient Selection**

**Suitable for Heart Valve PIFU**

  - Aged 18 or over.

  - Patient or carer understands and accepts the meaning and principles of a PIFU pathway.

  - Bicuspid aortic valve with non-severe valve disease.

  - Mitral valve prolapse with non-severe valve disease.

  - Moderate mitral valve disease and moderate aortic regurgitation.

  - Low to moderate aortic stenosis (Vmax <3.5 m/s with preserved left ventricular function).

  - Normally functioning prosthetic valves, bioprosthetic valves, mechanical heart valves.

  - Valve repair, percutaneous valvular intervention including TAVI and TEER.

**NOT Suitable for Heart Valve PIFU**

  - Patients with SEVERE valvular disease.

  - Patients with SYMPTOMATIC valvular disease.

  - Patients with LEFT VENTRICULAR IMPAIRMENT.

  - Patients with outstanding investigations or decisions still to be made.

  - Patients with complex HVD or who would benefit from regular review of clinical status.

  - Patients who do not have the knowledge, skills and confidence to manage follow-up.

  - Patients who cannot easily contact the service (no phone/internet, language barriers).

  - Patients deemed by their clinician to be unsuitable for valvular intervention (e.g. frailty, multiple co-morbidities) — these should be discharged back to their GP.

**Surveillance Imaging Intervals by Condition**

**Bicuspid Aortic Valve (normally functioning or mildly diseased)**

Imaging interval: 2-5 years (12 months if aortic dilatation >40mm). Echocardiogram.

Leave PIFU if: New/worsening symptoms; patient/staff concern; new dysrhythmia; new LV/RV dysfunction; significant aortic dilatation; progression to moderate valve disease; PA systolic pressure >50mmHg.

Note: Association of aortopathy with this condition. Consider cross-sectional imaging to check for underlying aortopathy at baseline. If echocardiogram cannot assess aortopathy alone, patient is NOT suitable for PIFU.

**Low Moderate Aortic Stenosis (Vmax <3.5 m/s, preserved LVEF, normal flow)**

Imaging interval: 1-2 years. Echocardiogram.

Leave PIFU if: New/worsening symptoms; patient/staff concern; new dysrhythmia; new LV/RV dysfunction; Aortic valve Vmax >3.5m/s or AVA <1.2cm2; PA systolic pressure >50mmHg.

Note: Due to the natural history of moderate-to-severe AS, a conservative threshold of "low moderate" is advised. Patients with low flow conditions should be excluded from PIFU.

**Moderate Aortic Regurgitation (preserved LVEF)**

Imaging interval: 1-2 years. Echocardiogram.

Leave PIFU if: New/worsening symptoms; patient/staff concern; new dysrhythmia; left ventricular dilatation; new LV/RV dysfunction; progression to severe AR; aortic dimensions >=40mm; PA systolic pressure >50mmHg.

Note: Association of aortopathy with this condition. Consider cross-sectional imaging to check for aortopathy at baseline.

**Mitral Valve Prolapse (no or mild regurgitation)**

Imaging interval: 3-5 years. Echocardiogram.

Leave PIFU if: New/worsening symptoms; patient/staff concern; new dysrhythmia; new LV/RV dysfunction; LV dilatation; increase in MR to moderate; PA systolic pressure >50mmHg.

**Moderate Mitral Regurgitation (preserved LVEF, PA pressure <50mmHg)**

Imaging interval: 18 months to 2 years. Echocardiogram.

Leave PIFU if: New/worsening symptoms; patient/staff concern; new dysrhythmia; new LV/RV dysfunction or LVEF <60%; LV dilatation; progression of MR to severe; PA systolic pressure >50mmHg; new tricuspid regurgitation (>=moderate).

**Mild to Moderate Mitral Stenosis (MVA >1.5 cm2)**

Imaging interval: 2-3 years. Echocardiogram.

Leave PIFU if: New/worsening symptoms; patient/staff concern; new dysrhythmia; new PA systolic pressure >50mmHg; new tricuspid regurgitation (>=moderate); new RV dysfunction; mitral valve area <1.5cm2.

Note: Some patients with MS with MVA <1.5cm2 will display symptoms and need closer clinical evaluation — NOT suitable for PIFU.

**Normally Functioning TAVI, Bioprosthetic Valve, Mitral Valve Repair, Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair (TEER)**

Imaging interval: 1-2 years. Echocardiogram.

Leave PIFU if: New/worsening symptoms; new dysrhythmia; patient/staff concern; new LV/RV dysfunction; new LV dilatation; significant increase in mean valve gradient vs previous echo (>=10mmHg); new tricuspid regurgitation (>=moderate); new valve prosthesis regurgitation (>=moderate); new PA pressure >50mmHg.

Note: Ensure routine post-operative TTE performed before entering patient onto PIFU so there is a reference scan. Some units may start imaging surveillance of bioprosthetic valves only after 5-10 years. Risk factors for disease progression (young age, smokers, diabetes, renal disease) may require more regular imaging. Patients with abnormal baseline echo should NOT be considered for PIFU.

**Mechanical Valve Prosthesis (no associated aortopathy)**

Imaging interval: No imaging follow-up required.

Note: Ensure routine post-operative TTE performed before PIFU. Patients with bicuspid aortic valve or co-existent untreated valve lesions may not be suitable. Consider TTE at 5 years in isolated mechanical MVR to assess right heart and tricuspid regurgitation.

**Target Response Times**

  - Patients with urgent problems should be evaluated within 2 WEEKS.

  - The responsible clinician decides if patient requires any tests and/or a consultation.

  - A robust audit process should be in place to monitor turnaround time between patient contact and clinical review.

**Design Considerations**

**What Patients on PIFU Need to Do**

Patients on a PIFU pathway alongside imaging surveillance should contact the HVD service if their health condition changes. This review should re-evaluate whether PIFU is still suitable.

**Managing the Surveillance Imaging Pathway**

  - The responsible clinician determines the imaging surveillance interval for each specific valve condition.

  - Local SOPs need to define imaging follow-up intervals for specific valve conditions.

  - Procedures must ensure surveillance imaging occurs at regular intervals; if cancelled or DNA, protocols must ensure patient is not lost in the system.

**Shared Decision-Making Must Cover**

  - Why the patient is suitable for a heart valve PIFU pathway.

  - How and when to contact the heart valve PIFU helpline, with contact phone number.

  - Red flag symptoms indicating progression or decompensation of HVD (e.g. endocarditis symptoms).

  - The importance of good dental and skin hygiene (risk of endocarditis).

  - Lifestyle advice: pre-pregnancy planning for females of childbearing age, sport and exercise, dental antibiotic prophylaxis, healthy lifestyle.

  - Clarification that the patient can change their mind and revert to traditional follow-up.

**Health Inequalities**

  - Systems should develop a cardiac network-based heart valve PIFU pathway to standardise management across a region.

  - Suitable non-severe HVD patients should be placed on PIFU to free up capacity for severe and complex valve disease.

  - Heart valve services should undertake an equalities and health inequalities impact assessment (EHIA).

**What is Patient Initiated Follow-Up (PIFU)?**

PIFU gives patients control over their follow-up care, allowing them to be seen quickly when they need to, while avoiding the inconvenience of appointments that are of low clinical value. This is a key part of the focus on empowering patients and delivering personalised care in the NHS.

PIFU describes when a patient, or carer, can initiate follow-up appointments when they need one. By allowing them to initiate appointments, patients can be seen quickly when their symptoms or circumstances change, and otherwise avoid unnecessary appointments.

PIFU commonly goes by other names including: open access follow-up, patient led follow-up, patient triggered follow-up, supported self-managed follow-up, open appointments, and patient activated care.

**Three Minimum Quality Standards for PIFU**

  - All patients and/or carers should be provided with appropriate and detailed information on PIFU, including condition-specific information such as what symptoms to look out for that would trigger an appointment. Clinicians are encouraged to hold a shared decision-making conversation.

  - A standard operating procedure (SOP) that includes patient safety nets should be in place.

  - All patients moved to a PIFU pathway should be logged and tracked on the organisation's IT system, and the service able to report on key metrics.

**Policy Context**

The 2024/25 Elective Reform Plan asks systems and providers to:

  - Ensure PIFU is offered as standard in all appropriate pathways by March 2026.

  - Significantly increase the uptake of PIFU to at least 5% of all outpatient appointments, including through the enhanced identification of suitable patients using AI and automation by March 2029.

**General Patient Selection Criteria**

**Inclusion – patients suitable for PIFU must:**

  - Be at low risk of urgent follow-up care and satisfy criteria established by the specialty.

  - Have the health literacy and knowledge, skills and confidence to manage their follow-up care.

  - Be confident and able to take responsibility for their care for the time they will be on the PIFU pathway, for example, they do not have rapidly progressing dementia, severe memory loss or a severe learning disability.

  - Understand which changes in their symptoms mean they should get in touch with the service, and how to do so.

  - Have the devices, leaflets and apps they need to understand the status of their condition.

  - Understand how to book follow-up appointments directly with the service; for some patients who are unable to book their appointments directly, administrative staff at their care home or GP surgery may be able to help.

**Exclusion – carefully consider if patient:**

  - Has particularly complex health issues.

  - Has clinical requirements to see the patient on a fixed timescale (timed follow-ups) — although a blend of PIFU and timed follow-ups can be offered.

  - Has safeguarding concerns raised by the clinician.

  - Takes medicines that require regular and robust monitoring in secondary care.

  - Is unable to contact the service easily (e.g. lacks access to a telephone or internet).

**Note: PIFU is NOT a substitute for discharge**

PIFU should never be used as an alternative to clinical discharge. Individual services should develop their own guidance, criteria and protocols on when to use PIFU and be clear that it should not be used as a substitute for the appropriate timely discharge of patients.

**Specialties Where PIFU Is Actively Used**

PIFU is suitable across a wide range of specialties and should be offered as the standard model of care across all appropriate specialties. It is particularly popular in trauma and orthopaedics and physiotherapy. Specialties with increasing usage include:

  - Cardiology

  - Audiology, Breast surgery, Colorectal, Dermatology, Diabetes

  - ENT, Endocrinology, Gastroenterology, General and vascular surgery

  - Geriatric medicine, Gynaecology, Hepatology, Mental health, Neurology

  - Oncology, Ophthalmology, Paediatrics, Pain management, Palliative medicine

  - Physiotherapy, Rheumatology, Respiratory, Thoracic medicine, Trauma and orthopaedics

**Patient Safety Requirements**

**Tracking and Safety Nets**

Providers must ensure:

  - Robust processes are in place to log patients on a PIFU pathway, including the review or end date for the PIFU window.

  - Clinicians identify patients who will need a review at the end of their PIFU window.

  - Patients who have been clinically assessed at their last appointment, or who have reached the end of their PIFU period without activating, can be discharged without a review (GPs must be informed).

  - Patients on a PIFU pathway are seen at a frequency that complies with clinical guidance (e.g. annual reviews for long-term conditions).

  - High risk patients who do not attend appointments are contacted to rebook.

  - Tests (bloods, scans) are reviewed in a timely way even if a patient does not initiate an appointment.

**Key Risks and Mitigations**

**Risk: Patient moved to PIFU without adequate knowledge/skills**

  - Local service guidelines should include patient suitability criteria and involve families/carers where appropriate.

  - The decision to go on the PIFU pathway should be a shared decision with the patient.

  - Patients should be given the option to return to timed follow-up if PIFU is not working for them.

**Risk: Patient forgets how/when to get in touch**

  - Send information both digitally and hard copy.

  - Keep patients' GPs informed so they can signpost the patient to the service.

  - Develop good communication resources such as patient information leaflets.

**Risk: Patient moved to PIFU when they require timed follow-ups**

  - Local service guidelines should state which conditions PIFU can be used with.

  - SOPs should ensure patients are seen at a frequency complying with clinical guidance.

**Inviting Patients to PIFU Outside of an Appointment (Asynchronously)**

Patients can be invited to a PIFU pathway without the need of a formal appointment — e.g. through waiting list validation or clinical review. If services choose to invite patients outside of an appointment, the following standards must be met:

  - There must be clinical approval or oversight to ensure the patient is appropriate.

  - Information on PIFU, including what it is and how to arrange an appointment if needed, must be shared with the patient in an accessible format with health literacy in mind.

  - Contact details for the service must be included to enable patients to ask questions.

  - Appropriate SOPs must be developed locally, including information on defaulting patients who do not respond back to a regular pathway.

**Health Inequalities**

PIFU is not a "one size fits all" approach. Key considerations:

  - All groups should be given equal opportunity to be offered PIFU if clinically appropriate.

  - If some patients are likely to struggle to contact the service (e.g. no phone or internet), consider whether suitable mitigation can be put in place so they can still be offered PIFU.

  - When implementing digital PIFU, patient communication preferences must be noted and acted on appropriately to avoid digital exclusion.

  - SOPs should include how to manage patients from minority groups who may need additional support.

**Summary: PIFU vs Discharge vs Timed Follow-Up**

PIFU should not be confused with either discharge or timed follow-up:

  - Discharge: Patient is returned to their GP with no ongoing outpatient specialist involvement. This is the preferred outcome for patients who are stable and no longer require specialist review.

  - Timed (scheduled) follow-up: Patient requires appointments at regular clinical intervals — appropriate where clinical need mandates review at fixed points (e.g. medication monitoring, post-intervention checks).

  - PIFU: Patient is stable but may need to access the specialist service when symptoms change. Patient initiates contact. Suitable where the condition has an uncertain trajectory or where patient-triggered access is safer than routine appointments.

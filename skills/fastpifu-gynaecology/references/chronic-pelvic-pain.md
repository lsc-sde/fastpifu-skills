# Chronic pelvic pain

<!-- author: derived from GIRFT Clinically-led Gynaecology Outpatient Guide (July 2023) | reviewed_by: <lead> | last_updated: 2026-09 -->

## Scope

Chronic pelvic pain (cyclical or non-cyclical) managed in secondary-care gynaecology, following
initial assessment and treatment. Excludes new/acute presentations requiring diagnostic work-up.

## PIFU — suitable for patient-initiated follow-up

- Criterion: PIFU suitable following successful treatment.
  Supported when: letter confirms pain is controlled/acceptable to the patient on the current
  management plan.

## RETAIN_FOLLOW_UP — timed specialist follow-up still needed

- Treatment being escalated or titrated, with response not yet confirmed.
- Ongoing pain-team or MDT involvement.
- Diagnosis unclear or investigations pending (e.g. imaging, diagnostic laparoscopy).

## DISCHARGE — no ongoing specialist follow-up needed

- Symptoms resolved with no ongoing specialist need, or condition better managed through a
  community/primary-care pain-management pathway.

## Borderline / uncertain (resolve to RETAIN_FOLLOW_UP, lower confidence)

- Pain described as "manageable" without confirmation that it is acceptable to the patient or that
  no further escalation is planned.

## Data that must be present to assess

| Required data point | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Pain severity/impact | Confirms symptom control |
| Treatment trialled and response | Determines stability |
| Underlying diagnosis (if established) | Identifies condition-specific pathway (e.g. endometriosis) |

## Surveillance / review interval

PIFU 6 months; trigger for return appointment is persisting symptoms; remote appointment type.

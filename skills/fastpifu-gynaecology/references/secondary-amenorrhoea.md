# Secondary amenorrhoea

<!-- concept_ids: (completed by data team) -->
<!-- author: derived from GIRFT Clinically-led Gynaecology Outpatient Guide (July 2023) | reviewed_by: <lead> | last_updated: 2026-09 -->

## Scope

Secondary amenorrhoea (absence of menses for ≥6 months in a previously menstruating patient)
following initial secondary-care work-up including blood sample analysis.

## PIFU — suitable for patient-initiated follow-up

- Criterion: PIFU suitable following blood sample analysis.
  Supported when: letter documents the hormonal profile (e.g. FSH/LH, prolactin, TSH, oestradiol)
  has been reviewed and a cause identified and managed, or a benign/functional cause excluded.

## RETAIN_FOLLOW_UP — timed specialist follow-up still needed

- Investigations pending or inconclusive (results not yet available or cause not established).
- Underlying cause requires ongoing titration of treatment (e.g. HRT, dopamine agonist for
  hyperprolactinaemia, treatment for premature ovarian insufficiency).

## DISCHARGE — no ongoing specialist follow-up needed

- Cause identified and resolved, or manageable entirely in primary care, with no ongoing
  specialist role.

## Borderline / uncertain (resolve to RETAIN_FOLLOW_UP, lower confidence)

- Blood results reviewed but no clear statement of cause or management plan.

## Data that must be present to assess

| Required data point | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Hormonal profile results (FSH/LH, prolactin, TSH, oestradiol) | Establishes cause |
| Cause identified | Determines PIFU vs discharge vs ongoing titration |
| Current management and response | Distinguishes stable PIFU from active titration |

## Surveillance / review interval

PIFU 6 months; trigger for return appointment is persisting symptoms; remote appointment type.
